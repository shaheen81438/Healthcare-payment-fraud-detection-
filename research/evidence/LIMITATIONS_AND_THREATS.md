# Limitations and threats to validity

1. **Synthetic targets and domain shift.** Labels encode configured F01–F12 behaviors. Results measure recognition of injected patterns, not Medicare fraud, adjudicated overpayments, or CMS CERT outcomes. Synthetic behavior prevalence, payment proxies and benign confounders are assumptions.
2. **No external validation.** There are no authorized real claim-line outcomes or verified investigative dispositions in the repository. CMS provider aggregates do not supply claim labels; the official OIG LEIE list is absent.
3. **Artifact reproducibility.** Frozen predictions and labels are available and match one-to-one, but model bundles and pipeline feature stores are missing. Recomputing the score metrics is possible; reproducing the fit, calibration and saved predictions from source is not currently possible.
4. **Threshold operating point.** At saved cutoff 0.9945 the synthetic FNR is 76.8132%. The threshold reflects a documented capacity/cost policy, not a medically or operationally validated optimum. Review capacity, recovery rates and false-positive costs are scenario assumptions.
5. **Score comparison is not causal ablation.** Rule, supervised, anomaly and ensemble score columns can be compared on a common synthetic label set; no component removal/retraining experiment has been recreated.
6. **Provider anomaly labels absent.** CMS group priorities are distribution-based review signals; a high score is not misconduct. Sample selection is only 40 service rows per specialty and is not a representative population evaluation.
7. **Payment rule input gaps.** No expected-payment/documentation fields occur in the local demo claims artifact, so the dedicated rules return no findings there. Rules need independently sourced expected amounts, applicable policy and documentation evidence.
8. **LEIE freshness and identity.** No official LEIE data were evaluated. OIG notes the full active-exclusion download changes monthly and reinstatements are removed; operational screening needs current source files and date/reinstatement synchronization. A name or NPI match is not proof of fraud and requires identity verification.
9. **Explainability provenance.** Existing saved SHAP importance is not regenerated from current model artifacts. Rule reasons and SHAP contributions are different explanation mechanisms.
10. **Dataset sample and year.** CMS local snapshot metadata gives retrieval date and row filters but not a reliable publication year for the sample; the exact year must not be inferred. The local CSV is a small calibration sample, not the full CMS release.
11. **Performance metrics.** Average precision is used as PR-AUC. Results depend on this synthetic class prevalence. Accuracy is inflated by class imbalance and is not the main objective. FNR and false-negative counts should guide review-capacity policy discussions but cannot be interpreted as real-world missed-fraud estimates.
12. **Selection and implementation risks.** Synthetic generator, rule assumptions, feature choices and temporal split are project-designed; broader provider, geography, care-setting, code and time coverage may change ranking behavior. No fairness, prospective workflow, security, or clinical impact study is part of this evidence run.
